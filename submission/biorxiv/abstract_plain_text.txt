Persistent homology applied to PCA-reduced omics data is increasingly invoked as evidence that gene-expression, methylation, or proteomic landscapes contain nontrivial topological structure beyond what a simple cluster or trajectory model would capture. This inference is rarely checked against a null model matched to the same preprocessing pipeline. Without such a comparison, an apparent first-homology signal in PCA space can be a generic artifact of dimensionality reduction on high-dimensional point clouds, real or null.

We tested two protocol-locked hypotheses across four independent cohorts, five cohort/layer analyses, four tissue types, and three omics modalities: bulk RNA-seq, DNA methylation, and mass-spectrometry proteomics. The first hypothesis asked whether real-data maximum H1 persistence significantly exceeds matched Gaussian and pipeline-symmetric permutation nulls in raw and PCA-reduced space. The second asked whether the PCA-driven inflation of persistence in real data exceeds the inflation induced by the same PCA step in null data.

Across 16 confirmatory BH-FDR-corrected tests, the real-vs-null signal was supported in 14 of 16 comparisons. The two failures occurred in one feature space, TCGA-LUAD methylation after PCA, where the real signal itself collapsed to zero. The PCA-attribution test held at the preregistered operating point across the tested cohorts, but an 18-configuration ablation sweep showed clear boundary conditions: the result fails at very low principal-component count and at one high-gene-count/high-PC-count setting against the pipeline-symmetric null.

We further tested whether detected topological signals were independent of tumor/normal class separability using within-class decomposition, within-stratum controls, residualization, and block-bootstrap confidence intervals. The result was dataset-dependent. In GSE81089 and CPTAC-CCRCC, the signal was not attributable to the tumor/normal class-mean shift by every control tested. In GSE146889 and TCGA-LUAD, the evidence was weaker or partially confound-entangled, with TCGA-LUAD collapsing to null-indistinguishable specifically in the primary PCA(50) space after confound residualization.

These results support a bounded methodological conclusion: PCA-space persistent-homology signals in omics data require pipeline-matched null interpretation, and confound independence must be audited per dataset rather than assumed from a single successful case.
